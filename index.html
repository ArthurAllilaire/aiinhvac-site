AI in HVAC: the best AI for HVAC technicians in 2026

By Arthur Allilaire, co-founder and CTO of Kenner · Vendor facts checked on 10 November 2026

There is no single best AI for HVAC techs: the right one depends on the job. Troubleshooting from manuals and guides is now a commodity. The real gains come from capturing your own field knowledge and getting AI to take action as well as give advice. This guide compares 14 tools based on published content.

The short answer

Pick on three things: does it take action or only advise, will technicians actually use it, and does your field knowledge become a company asset. For a commercial HVAC or refrigeration service team that is Kenner, which we make. If your shop runs on ServiceTitan, BuildOps or ServiceTrade, start with the AI already in it. For a single technician, the manufacturers' free apps, measureQuick, HVAC/R AI Pro and ChatGPT cover a lot, as long as the answer is checked against the manual.

  • General questions, at no cost: ChatGPT

Questions and answers

What is the best AI for HVAC technicians?

It depends on the job. For a commercial HVAC/R service team that wants AI that takes action and captures its own know-how, Kenner, which we make. For a shop on ServiceTitan, Atlas. For model-specific troubleshooting across brands, Bluon. For diagnosis from live readings, measureQuick. For a free start, the manufacturers' apps and ChatGPT.

What is the best AI for HVAC techs who work alone?

Start with what is free: the measureQuick app, Copeland Mobile, the Carrier Service Technician app and the free plan of ChatGPT or HVAC/R AI Pro. If you pay for one, HVAC/R AI Pro is $10 to $20 a month and measureQuick's AI is in its $49 a month tier.

Can I use ChatGPT for HVAC troubleshooting?

Yes, for general questions, and it takes photos and voice. It has not seen the history of the unit and it can be wrong about a specific model, and OpenAI itself advises verifying technical information. Check anything it says against the manual before you act on it.

Is there a free AI for HVAC technicians?

Yes. Copeland Mobile with Scout AI and the Carrier Service Technician app are free, ChatGPT and HVAC/R AI Pro have free plans, and the measureQuick app is free, though its AI is in the paid tier.

Which AI knows what was done to this unit before?

Only tools connected to your company's records. Kenner answers from your own units and past jobs on any system. Atlas, BuildOps OpsAI and ServiceTrade use the history inside their own platforms. Apps for individual technicians and general chatbots do not have it.

Why does our own service data matter for AI?

The models are available to everyone. Your service history is not. The best AI comes from the best data, so a knowledge base built from your own jobs is an asset that sets you apart from competitors, and it compounds: the sooner you start, the more it knows. Kenner, which we make, builds that knowledge base for your company as technicians use it.

Will AI replace HVAC technicians?

No. Every tool here gives a technician information or takes paperwork off them. Recovering refrigerant, brazing a joint and deciding whether a unit is safe to run stay with a certified person on site.

Does it matter whether the AI needs an app?

On a roof with gloves on, yes, and it decides whether technicians use it at all. We make Kenner, and in more than 200 conversations we have had with service leaders, two said getting technicians to use new apps had not been a struggle. Kenner works in WhatsApp, iMessage, Teams, RCS, SMS or a phone call, and Lennox's assistant works by text message. Most of the others are an app or a tab inside the field service app.

Can I call an AI for HVAC help?

Yes. Kenner answers an ordinary phone call and draws on your own units' history. ChatGPT has a phone line in the US and Canada, 1-800-CHATGPT, but it is a general chatbot with no link to your records. Lennox's agent works by text message. The rest work inside their own apps.

What about AI for HVAC companies, such as call answering and scheduling?

That is a different job. AI receptionists, dispatch and marketing tools help the office, and this guide leaves them out: it covers AI that helps the technician on the job.
